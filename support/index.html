Connecting a bank

Connecting a bank is optional — Nami also works from statements you upload. The connection is made through TrueLayer, the open banking provider authorised and regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority; you choose your bank from TrueLayer's list and log in at your bank's own app or website, so Nami never sees your banking password. The access Nami asks for is read-only: it can read your accounts, balances, cards and transactions, and it cannot make a payment or move money. Bank linking is part of Pro, and you start a connection from the + on Net Wealth.

  • If a connection says "Needs reconnecting" — go to Profile → Connected banks → Reconnect. Reconnect is not Refresh: Refresh now asks your bank for today's balances under the permission you already gave, while Reconnect walks the whole consent again. Reconnect is also what to use when a card or your transactions never appear, because the permissions are fixed at the moment you authorise them and only a new consent can widen them.
  • To hide an account, and to get it back — swipe the account's card on Net Wealth and choose Hide this account. It stays off Net Wealth until you show it again, and the bank stays connected throughout. Bring it back from Profile → Connected banks → Hidden accounts → Show.
  • To disconnect — Profile → Connected banks → Disconnect. That removes the whole connection from Nami: its accounts, its balances and the tokens behind it, so nothing further can be fetched from your bank. Transactions already synced stay in your account. Your bank keeps its own list of who you have shared with, so withdraw the consent there too if you want it gone at both ends.

Statements

You can upload a PDF or CSV bank statement instead of connecting a bank, or as well as. Statement uploads are on by default and switch themselves off once a bank is connected — the switch is Profile → Statements → Statement uploads, and your own answer sticks whatever you do with your bank connections afterwards. If you turn uploads back on while a bank is connected, Nami warns you first: a statement for an account the bank is already feeding counts that spending twice.

Nami shows you the file picker first, then the AI processing disclosure, and nothing is sent until you agree. What is sent is the content of the file — transaction dates, merchant names, amounts, and any text in the statement — so whatever is printed on the statement, such as your name or an account number, goes with it.

Income and shifts

Nami reads your shifts from one calendar on your device. Nothing leaves your phone: the rota itself is never uploaded. Set it up from Income → Set up your employer, which asks for the calendar, the words your shift titles start with, your rate and unpaid break rule, and your pay cycle.

If a figure differs from your rota app, it is almost always one of two things. Nami reads the times on the rostered calendar event — start to end, less your unpaid break — not the hours written into the event's notes and not the time you actually clocked. And if the hours look right but the total does not, check the pay-cycle rule: work in the back half of a month is often paid at the end of the following one, and the gap is usually the cutoff rather than the hours.

Budgets and categories

The monthly budget is free for everyone — set it from Profile → Budgeting → Monthly budget. One category spending limit is free as well; Pro sets a limit on every category you spend in. A limit you have already set stays editable and removable on any plan.

Your categories live in Profile → Budgeting → Categories: free includes one of your own, Pro is unlimited. Built-in categories cannot be renamed. Category Rules, in the same section, is where a merchant you have recategorised is remembered, so the next transaction from them lands in the right place on its own.

Nami Pro

Your income from one employer, and your Net Wealth, are free for everyone. Pro adds the spending side and up to four employers: unlimited statement analysis where free reads one a month, a limit on every category, every month you have had rather than only the one you are in, fifty Ask Nami questions a day rather than five, bank connections, the Sankey money flow, Nami's Picks, unlimited categories of your own, merchant renaming that sticks, and the savings projector.

When your Apple ID is eligible for one, Pro opens with a free trial of 14 days; the paywall states the length and the price that follows before you commit, and a subscription renews automatically unless it is cancelled at least 24 hours before the period ends. Manage or cancel it in Settings → Apple ID → Subscriptions. If you have paid before and the app has not caught up — a reinstall, or a new device — use Restore Purchases at the bottom of the Pro screen.

Notifications

Everything Nami can send is in Profile → Notifications: a morning brief, an evening sum-up of what you spent, budget alerts, a payday-eve nudge, your pay the moment a shift ends, and repayment reminders. Each has its own switch, so you can keep one and turn the rest off. If notifications for Nami are switched off in iOS Settings, nothing on that screen can be delivered — it says so at the top and links you there.

Your data

What Nami collects, where it goes and why is set out in full in our privacy policy.

To remove everything, open Profile and tap Delete Account at the bottom of the screen. That deletes your account and, with it, your transactions, your uploaded statements, your budgets, category limits and rules, and any bank connection along with the tokens behind it. It cannot be undone. If you would rather clear your statements and keep your account, use Delete All Statements under Profile → Privacy & Data — that removes the uploads and their transactions and leaves your account and settings as they are. The same section is where you can re-read the AI processing disclosure and change your answer to it.

Nami does not currently offer a data export.
