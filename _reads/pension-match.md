sections:
  - heading: Who gets one
    text: |-
      If you are 22 or over, under State Pension age, work in the UK and earn more than £10,000 a year from one job, your employer has to put you into a workplace pension. That is automatic enrolment.

      If you earn less, you can usually still ask to join, and above £6,240 a year your employer still has to pay in.
  - heading: The minimum
    text: |-
      At least 8% of your **qualifying earnings** goes in. For 2026 to 2027 those are your earnings between £6,240 and £50,270 a year.

      - Your employer pays at least 3%.
      - You pay the rest, at least 5%, and that 5% includes tax relief.
  - heading: A worked example
    text: |-
      On a salary of £30,000 a year:

      - Qualifying earnings: £30,000 minus £6,240 = £23,760
      - 8% in total: £1,900.80 a year, £158.40 a month
      - Your employer: 3%, £59.40 a month
      - Your 5%: £99 a month, of which about £19.80 is tax relief

      So about £158 a month goes into your pension, and your take-home pay drops by about £79. That gap is the employer's money plus tax relief.
  - heading: The match
    text: |-
      Many employers pay more than the 3% minimum, often matching what you put in up to a limit. For example, "we match up to 6%" usually means:

      - You pay 4%: they pay 4%
      - You pay 6%: they pay 6%
      - You pay 8%: they still pay 6%

      Each scheme words this differently. Some count your full pay and some only basic pay or qualifying earnings. Your contract, staff handbook or the pension provider's app will say how yours works. The match is part of your pay package, just paid later.
  - heading: How the tax relief shows
    text: |-
      - **Net pay arrangement**: your contribution comes off before tax, so your tax line is lower. If you earn under the Personal Allowance you may miss out on relief this way.
      - **Relief at source**: your contribution comes off after tax, and the provider claims 20% for you. Higher rate taxpayers can claim the rest through HMRC.
      - **Salary sacrifice**: your gross pay is lowered and your employer pays it all in, saving tax and National Insurance.
  - heading: Opting out
    text: |-
      You can leave. If you opt out within a month of being enrolled you get back what you paid in. But your employer's contribution stops too, so you give up that part of your pay.

      Your employer has to enrol you again about every three years if you still qualify, and you can ask to rejoin at any time.
