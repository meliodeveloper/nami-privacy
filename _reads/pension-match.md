sections:
  - heading: Who gets one
    text: >-
      If you are 22 or over, earn more than £10,000 a year from one job and work in
      the UK, your employer has to enrol you in a workplace pension. You can join
      even if you earn less.
  - heading: The minimum
    text: >-
      At least 8% of your qualifying earnings goes in each pay. Your employer pays
      at least 3% of that, and you pay the rest, including tax relief.
  - heading: The match
    text: >-
      Some employers pay more than 3%, often matching what you put in up to a
      limit. That extra is part of your pay, just paid later. Your contract or
      staff handbook says how your employer's scheme works.
  - heading: Tax relief
    text: >-
      Pension contributions get tax relief, so £100 into your pension usually costs
      you less than £100 of take-home pay. How it shows on your payslip depends on
      how the scheme takes it.
  - heading: Opting out
    text: >-
      You can leave, but your employer's contribution stops too. If you leave, you
      are usually enrolled again about every three years, and you can choose to
      rejoin at any time.
