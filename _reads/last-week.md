sections:
  - heading: Count from payday
    text: >-
      Your month starts when money arrives, not on the 1st. A budget set from
      payday to payday matches the money you actually have.
  - heading: Take the fixed bills out first
    text: >-
      Rent, bills and subscriptions are spoken for. What is left is what you
      really have to spend, and it is usually smaller than it feels on payday.
  - heading: Why the first week runs fast
    text: >-
      The first days after payday tend to carry the big shop, a night out and
      anything you put off. That is normal. The trouble starts when the same pace
      carries on, because the last week then pays for it.
  - heading: A daily figure helps
    text: >-
      Dividing what is left by the days left gives you one number to hold each
      day. If one day goes over, the next few days come down a little, instead of
      the last week taking the whole hit. This is what Nami's daily budget does.
  - heading: Leave a little room
    text: >-
      Something always comes up: a birthday, a train fare, a broken charger. A
      small amount set aside for things you can't name yet is what stops one
      surprise from becoming an overdraft.
