sections:
  - heading: Count from payday
    text: |-
      Your month starts when your money arrives, not on the 1st. A budget set from payday to payday matches the money you actually have, so the numbers line up with your bank account.
  - heading: Take the fixed bills out first
    text: |-
      Rent, bills, phone, travel passes and subscriptions are already spoken for. What is left is what you really have to spend, and it is usually smaller than it feels on payday.

      Here is an example:

      - Take-home pay: £2,000
      - Rent and bills: £1,100
      - **Left to spend: £900 over 30 days, about £30 a day**
  - heading: Why the first week runs fast
    text: |-
      The days after payday tend to carry the big food shop, a night out, and anything you put off until you were paid. That is normal.

      The trouble starts when the same pace carries on. In the example, spending £350 in the first week leaves £550 for 23 days. That is about £24 a day, down from £30. Nothing has gone wrong yet, but the last week will feel it.
  - heading: A daily figure helps
    text: |-
      Dividing what is left by the days left gives one number to hold each day.

      - Overspend by £10 today, and each of the remaining days comes down a little.
      - Spend less today, and the next days get a little more room.

      Small, early corrections are easier than one big squeeze at the end. This is what Nami's daily budget does for you.
  - heading: Plan for the costs that aren't monthly
    text: |-
      Many budgets break on costs that don't come every month: birthdays, Christmas, a car MOT, a yearly subscription. Adding them up and dividing by 12 gives you a monthly amount to set aside.

      - Christmas: £300
      - Birthdays: £240
      - Car MOT and service: £240
      - **Total £780 a year, or £65 a month**
  - heading: Leave a little room
    text: |-
      Something always comes up: a train fare, a broken charger, a leaving collection. A small amount set aside for things you can't name yet stops one surprise from becoming an overdraft.

      If you often run short, it can help to move payday spending into a separate pot. Then what is in your main account is only what you have to live on.
  - heading: If you do run short
    text: |-
      - Look at what is due before payday and when, so nothing bounces.
      - Talk to your bank before going past an overdraft limit.
