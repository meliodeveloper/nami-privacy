sections:
  - heading: How much comes off
    text: >-
      You repay 9% of what you earn above your plan's threshold, or 6% for a
      postgraduate loan. Below the threshold you repay nothing. Your employer takes
      it through payroll, so it shows on your payslip next to tax.
  - heading: Which plan you are on
    text: >-
      It depends on where you lived and when you started your course. Plan 1, 2, 4
      and 5 each have a different threshold, and the thresholds usually change
      each April. You can check your plan in your student loan account on GOV.UK.
  - heading: When it ends
    text: >-
      Whatever is left is written off after a set time, for example 30 years after
      you were first due to repay on Plan 2, or 40 years on Plan 5. Many people on
      these plans never repay the full amount, which is why it behaves more like a
      tax than a normal loan.
  - heading: Your credit file
    text: >-
      Student loans do not appear on your credit file. A mortgage lender may still
      count the monthly repayment as one of your outgoings.
  - heading: Paying more
    text: >-
      You can make extra payments, but you cannot get them back. Whether that makes
      sense depends on how much you are likely to repay in total, which depends on
      your plan and your future pay.
