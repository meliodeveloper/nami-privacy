sections:
  - heading: How much comes off
    text: |-
      You repay a share of what you earn **above your plan's threshold**, not a share of what you owe.

      - Plans 1, 2, 4 and 5: 9% of earnings over the threshold
      - Postgraduate loan: 6% of earnings over £21,000 a year

      Below the threshold you repay nothing. Your employer takes it through payroll, so it sits on your payslip next to tax and National Insurance.
  - heading: The thresholds for 2026 to 2027
    text: |-
      - **Plan 1**: £26,900 a year, £2,241 a month
      - **Plan 2**: £29,385 a year, £2,448 a month
      - **Plan 4**: £33,795 a year, £2,816 a month
      - **Plan 5**: £25,000 a year, £2,083 a month
      - **Postgraduate**: £21,000 a year, £1,750 a month

      Thresholds can change each April, so a pay rise and a threshold rise can cancel each other out.
  - heading: Two worked examples
    text: |-
      **Plan 2, earning £35,000 a year.** That is about £2,916.67 a month. Above the £2,448.75 threshold is £467.92, and 9% of that is £42.11. Payroll rounds down, so about £42 a month.

      **Plan 5, earning £30,000 a year.** That is £2,500 a month. Above the £2,083.33 threshold is £416.67, and 9% of that is £37.50. About £37 a month.

      The amount you owe makes no difference to either figure. Someone owing £20,000 and someone owing £60,000 on the same plan and pay repay the same each month.
  - heading: Which plan you are on
    text: |-
      It depends on where you lived and when your course started:

      - **Plan 1**: England or Wales before September 2012, or Northern Ireland
      - **Plan 2**: England or Wales, September 2012 to July 2023
      - **Plan 4**: Scotland
      - **Plan 5**: England, from August 2023

      You can have more than one plan. Your student loan account on GOV.UK shows yours.
  - heading: When it ends
    text: |-
      Whatever is left is written off after a set time, counted from the April you were first due to repay.

      - Plan 1, loans from September 2006: 25 years
      - Plan 2: 30 years
      - Plan 4, loans from August 2007: 30 years
      - Plan 5: 40 years

      Interest is added while you repay. Many people on Plans 2 and 5 never repay the full amount before it is written off. That is why it behaves more like a tax on earnings above a line than a normal loan.
  - heading: Your credit file and mortgages
    text: |-
      Student loans don't appear on your credit file, and they don't count as a missed payment if you earn below the threshold. A mortgage lender may still count the monthly repayment as one of your outgoings when it works out how much it will lend.
  - heading: Paying extra, and getting money back
    text: |-
      You can make extra payments, but you can't get them back. Whether that is worth it depends on how much you are likely to repay before write-off, which depends on your plan and your future pay.

      You can sometimes get money back:

      - If you earned under the yearly threshold but repayments were taken, for example from one big month
      - If you repaid more than you owed

      If you are self-employed, repayments go through your Self Assessment tax return instead of payroll.
