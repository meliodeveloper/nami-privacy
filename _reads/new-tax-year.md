sections:
  - heading: Why 6 April matters
    text: |-
      The UK tax year runs from 6 April to 5 April. Allowances reset, some thresholds change, and anything you haven't used by 5 April is usually gone. A few things are worth a look either side of the date.
  - heading: Your tax code
    text: |-
      HMRC may send a new code for the new year. Check the first payslip after 6 April shows the code you expect. For most people that is still 1257L, because the Personal Allowance stays at £12,570.

      If your code changes in April, check why in the HMRC app.
  - heading: ISAs
    text: |-
      Each tax year you can put up to £20,000 into ISAs, split across types as you like. Anything you don't use by 5 April is lost.

      - From 6 April 2027, if you are under 65, no more than £12,000 of the £20,000 can go into cash ISAs.
      - The rest can still go into stocks and shares, or other ISA types.
  - heading: Your P60
    text: |-
      Your employer has to give you a P60 by 31 May. It shows your total pay and tax for the year that has just ended. Keep it, because you may need it to:

      - Prove your income for a mortgage or a rental
      - Check whether you paid the right tax
      - Claim back tax you overpaid
  - heading: Marriage Allowance
    text: |-
      If one of you earns less than £12,570 and the other pays basic rate tax, the lower earner can pass £1,260 of their allowance to the other. That can cut the couple's tax by up to £252 a year.

      You can backdate a claim for up to four years, so it is worth checking even if it has applied for a while.
  - heading: Pensions
    text: |-
      You can usually pay up to £60,000 a year into pensions with tax relief, or up to what you earn if that is less. Your workplace pension's April statement is a good moment to check what is going in.
  - heading: Self Assessment
    text: |-
      If you are self-employed, a landlord, or have other income outside your job, your tax return for the year that just ended is due online by 31 January.

      - Filing early does not mean paying early. Payment is still due by 31 January.
      - Making Tax Digital for Income Tax started on 6 April 2026 for qualifying income over £50,000. It applies from 6 April 2027 if yours was over £30,000 in 2025 to 2026.
  - heading: A quick checklist
    text: |-
      - Check your new tax code on your first April payslip
      - Use any ISA allowance before 5 April
      - Keep your P60 when it arrives
      - Check whether Marriage Allowance applies to you
      - Note the 31 January Self Assessment deadline if it applies
