sections:
  - heading: The number
    text: >-
      Most codes are a number and a letter. The number is your tax-free allowance
      with the last digit dropped, so 1257L means you can earn £12,570 in the tax
      year before income tax starts. That is the standard Personal Allowance.
  - heading: The letter
    text: >-
      L means you get the standard allowance. M means your partner has passed you
      10% of their allowance through Marriage Allowance, and N means you passed 10%
      of yours to them. A code starting with S means you pay Scottish rates, and C
      means Welsh rates.
  - heading: Codes with no number
    text: >-
      0T means your allowance is used up, or your new employer does not have your
      details yet, so tax is taken from the first pound. BR means everything from
      this job is taxed at the basic rate of 20%, which is common on a second job.
      D0 means the higher rate of 40%. NT means no tax is taken.
  - heading: W1, M1, X or NONCUM at the end
    text: >-
      This is an emergency code. Each pay is taxed on its own, as if it were your
      first of the year, instead of looking at what you have earned and paid so
      far. You often see it when you start a new job before HMRC has your details.
  - heading: K at the start
    text: >-
      A K code means you have income that is not taxed anywhere else, such as a
      company car or tax owed from an earlier year, and it is more than your
      allowance. So it adds to your taxable pay instead of taking away from it.
  - heading: If yours looks wrong
    text: >-
      You can see your code, and why HMRC gave it, in the HMRC app or your personal
      tax account on GOV.UK, and update your details there. If too much tax was
      taken, it usually comes back through your pay once the code is fixed, or
      after the tax year ends.
