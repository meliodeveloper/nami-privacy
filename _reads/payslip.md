sections:
  - heading: Gross and net
    text: |-
      **Gross pay** is everything you earned in the pay period: your salary or hours, overtime, holiday pay, bonus and any allowances. **Net pay** is what lands in your account.

      Everything in between is a deduction. Most are worked out by your employer's payroll before the money leaves, so it helps to know what each line is.
  - heading: A worked example
    text: |-
      Here is a monthly payslip for someone earning £2,500 in a month, in England, on tax code 1257L, with a Plan 2 student loan, for 2026 to 2027.

      - Gross pay: £2,500.00
      - Income tax: about £290.50
      - National Insurance: about £116.16
      - Student loan: £4.00
      - **Net pay: about £2,089.34**

      So roughly £410 of £2,500 goes before payday. The sections below show where each figure comes from. A pension contribution would also come off, and the pension read explains it.
  - heading: Income tax
    text: |-
      Tax is worked out across the whole tax year, 6 April to 5 April. Your tax code tells payroll how much of each pay is tax-free.

      In England, Wales and Northern Ireland, for 2026 to 2027:

      - Up to £12,570: 0%, your Personal Allowance
      - £12,571 to £50,270: 20%, the basic rate
      - £50,271 to £125,140: 40%, the higher rate
      - Over £125,140: 45%, the additional rate

      Above £100,000, the allowance shrinks by £1 for every £2 earned, and it is gone at £125,140. Scotland has its own bands and rates.

      In the example, £1,047.50 of the £2,500 is tax-free. Tax at 20% on the other £1,452.50 is about £290.50.
  - heading: National Insurance
    text: |-
      National Insurance builds your record for the State Pension and some benefits. For 2026 to 2027 most employees pay:

      - Nothing on the first £1,048 a month
      - 8% on earnings from £1,048 to £4,189 a month
      - 2% on anything above £4,189 a month

      In the example, 8% of £1,452 (£2,500 minus £1,048) is about £116.16.

      Unlike tax, it is worked out pay by pay, not across the year. A month with a big bonus pays more National Insurance than the same money spread over the year.
  - heading: Student loan
    text: |-
      If you have one, payroll takes 9% of what you earn above your plan's threshold, or 6% above £21,000 a year for a postgraduate loan.

      On Plan 2 the monthly threshold is £2,448.75. In the example, 9% of £51.25 is £4.61. Repayments are rounded down to whole pounds, so it shows as £4.
  - heading: Pension
    text: |-
      If you are in a workplace pension, your share comes off here and your employer adds theirs on top. It is still your money, just for later. How it shows depends on the scheme:

      - **Net pay**: it comes off before tax is worked out, so your tax is lower.
      - **Relief at source**: it comes off after tax, and the pension provider claims the 20% tax relief for you.
      - **Salary sacrifice**: your gross pay is lowered, which saves tax and National Insurance.
  - heading: Other lines you may see
    text: |-
      - **Holiday pay** and **overtime**, often on their own lines
      - **Statutory Sick Pay** or parental pay
      - **Benefits in kind**, such as health cover, which can show for tax without adding cash
      - **Year to date** totals since 6 April
  - heading: Checking yours
    text: |-
      The year to date column is the quickest check. Compare tax so far with what you have earned so far. If your code is right and your pay is steady, tax each month should be similar.

      If something looks off, ask payroll first. Then check your tax code in the HMRC app.
