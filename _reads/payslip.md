sections:
  - heading: Gross and net
    text: >-
      Gross pay is everything you earned this period: hours, overtime, holiday pay
      and any bonus. Net pay is what lands in your account. Everything in between
      is a deduction, and most of them are worked out by your employer's payroll
      before the money leaves.
  - heading: Income tax
    text: >-
      Tax is worked out across the whole tax year, 6 April to 5 April. In England,
      Wales and Northern Ireland you pay 20% on earnings above your allowance up to
      £50,270, and 40% above that. Scotland has its own bands. Your tax code tells
      payroll how much allowance to give you each pay.
  - heading: National Insurance
    text: >-
      This builds your right to the State Pension and some benefits. For the
      2026 to 2027 tax year most employees pay 8% on monthly earnings between £1,048
      and £4,189, and 2% on anything above. Unlike tax, it is worked out pay by
      pay, so a big month costs more National Insurance than the same money spread
      out.
  - heading: Pension
    text: >-
      If you are enrolled in a workplace pension, your share comes off here and
      your employer adds theirs on top. It is money you keep, just later.
  - heading: Student loan
    text: >-
      If you have one, repayments are a fixed share of what you earn over your
      plan's threshold, taken through payroll.
  - heading: The year to date column
    text: >-
      Most payslips also show totals since 6 April. They are the quickest way to
      check that tax so far looks right for what you have earned so far.
