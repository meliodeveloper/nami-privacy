sections:
  - heading: One interest rate
    text: >-
      Since 2020, banks charge a single yearly interest rate on overdrafts, often
      around 40%. They can no longer add fixed daily or monthly fees on top, and
      arranged and unarranged overdrafts cost the same.
  - heading: What that looks like
    text: >-
      At around 40% a year, being £500 overdrawn for a month costs roughly £15. It
      adds up quietly because it is taken as interest, not as one fee you notice.
  - heading: Buffers
    text: >-
      Some banks give a small interest-free amount, such as the first £50 or £100.
      Your banking app or your bank's website says if yours does.
  - heading: Refused payments
    text: >-
      A bank can still charge if it turns down a payment because there is not
      enough money, though many no longer do.
  - heading: Your credit file
    text: >-
      Using an arranged overdraft shows on your credit file. Staying within your
      limit is fine. Going past it, or being in it all the time, is what lenders
      tend to notice.
  - heading: If you are in it every month
    text: >-
      That usually means the overdraft has become part of your monthly money. Your
      bank has to help if you ask, and free debt advice is available from
      MoneyHelper.
