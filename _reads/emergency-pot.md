sections:
  - heading: What it is for
    text: |-
      A broken boiler, a vet bill, a gap between jobs, a car that won't start. You can't plan when these happen, but you know something will. Without money set aside, they usually go on a card, an overdraft or a pay later plan, and cost more in the end.
  - heading: How big
    text: |-
      A common aim is three months of **essential** spending. That is what you would need to keep going, not what you usually spend.

      For example:

      - Rent: £750
      - Bills: £250
      - Food: £250
      - Getting to work: £150
      - **£1,400 a month, so about £4,200 for three months**

      That can feel a long way off. Starting smaller is fine. A first goal of £500 or £1,000 covers many of the surprises that come up in a year.
  - heading: Building it
    text: |-
      Moving money on payday, before you start spending, tends to work better than saving what is left at the end of the month.

      - £50 a month: £600 in a year, £1,000 in 20 months
      - £100 a month: £1,200 in a year
      - £25 a week: £1,300 in a year

      Any windfall, like a tax refund or a bonus, can jump it forward.
  - heading: Where to keep it
    text: |-
      Somewhere you can reach in a day or two, but separate from your spending account so it doesn't get spent by accident.

      - **Easy access savings** is the usual home. You can take money out when you need it.
      - **Notice accounts** may pay a little more, but you wait to get your money.
      - **Fixed term accounts** lock money away, which doesn't suit an emergency pot.

      The rate matters less than being able to reach it.
  - heading: Is it safe
    text: |-
      Money in a UK bank or building society is protected by the FSCS up to £120,000 per person, per banking licence, or £240,000 for a joint account. Some brands share one licence, so the limit covers them together.
  - heading: Tax on the interest
    text: |-
      Most people pay no tax on savings interest. The Personal Savings Allowance lets you earn interest tax-free each year:

      - Basic rate taxpayers: £1,000
      - Higher rate taxpayers: £500
      - Additional rate taxpayers: none

      A cash ISA keeps interest tax-free whatever you earn.
  - heading: Using it
    text: |-
      Taking money out of the pot for a real emergency is what it is for, not a failure. Once things settle, build it back up the same way you started.
