Nami: Spending Tracker

Privacy Policy

Overview

Nami: Spending Tracker ("we", "our", or "us") is a personal finance app that shows you where your money goes, what you have earned and what is on its way. It uses AI to categorise your transactions, to read the statements you upload, to answer the questions you ask it and to write your morning brief. Your transactions reach Nami in three ways: through a read-only open banking connection to a bank you choose to connect, from a bank statement you upload yourself, or from a purchase your own Tap to Log automation hands to Nami on your phone. This policy explains what data we collect, how we use it, which third parties receive it and why, and your rights.

Nami is made by Melio Labs Ltd, a company registered in England and Wales (company number 16136624). Melio Labs Ltd is the controller of your personal data under UK data protection law, and you can reach us at the address at the foot of this page.

AI At a glance: AI data processing

  • What we share: from a connected bank, the transactions our own rules could not categorise, each with its direction, amount, description and merchant. From a statement (PDF or CSV) you upload, the file as it is. When you ask Nami a question, your question and the figures it needs to answer it. Each morning, the figures your morning brief is built from.
  • Who it goes to: Anthropic, through its Claude models, always by way of our own server. The full breakdown is in the AI Processing section below.
  • What we never add: your bank login, your full card numbers, your account numbers, your email address or your password. A statement is sent as it is, so whatever is printed on it, such as your name or an account number, goes with it.
  • Retention: Anthropic does not use your data to train its models. It is deleted within 30 days in normal use, unless Anthropic has to keep it longer to enforce its usage rules or by law.
  • Your control: nothing is sent for AI processing until you agree. Your agreement is recorded against your account and checked again on our own server before every request. You can turn AI processing off at any time from Profile, Privacy & data, AI processing, while you are online (see AI Processing for why), and delete your account and the data Nami holds against it from Profile, Privacy & data, Delete account.

Data We Collect

  • Email address: used to create and manage your account. Provided either when you sign up with email and password, or returned to us by Apple or Google when you choose Sign in with Apple or Sign in with Google.
  • Account identifier: a unique opaque user ID is created for your account. Once you have signed in, it is the only identifier our analytics and our subscription provider know your account by. Before you sign in, each of them uses a random identifier of its own, which is joined to your account identifier when you sign in.
  • Your name, in two cases only: if you sign in with Google, the name and profile picture on your Google account are stored with your account. If you upload a statement, the account holder's name that Nami reads from it is stored with the transactions read from it. Nami asks for the first name only, but it stores what the reading returns.
  • Bank connection data: if you choose to connect a bank, we receive from that bank, through our open banking provider: the names and types of your accounts, their balances, your cards in masked form (the card's name and its last four digits only), and your transactions (date, description, merchant and amount). We never see or ask for your online banking login details, and we do not store your full card number, account number or sort code. This is fully described in Connecting a Bank (Open Banking) below.
  • Bank statement content: statement uploads are off until you turn them on. When you upload a PDF or CSV bank statement, the file is sent to our AI provider to extract and categorise your transactions, after you have agreed to AI processing. The transactions read from it are stored in your account with the bank's name.
  • Financial transaction data: the transactions synced from a connected bank, and those extracted from your statements, are stored securely in your account, along with the categories, merchant rules and merchant names you set.
  • Budgets and settings: your monthly budget, your category limits, the categories you create, your currency, your time zone, your morning brief time and whether Nami is drawn light or dark. Our server uses these to write your morning brief, to send budget alerts and to keep the Lock Screen cards up to date while the app is closed.
  • Income and shifts: for an employer you set up, its name, the words your shift titles start with, your hourly rate, your break rule, your pay day rules and which calendar holds your rota; for each shift you have worked, its start and end times, its hours, the pay Nami worked out for it and an internal reference to the calendar entry, so the same shift is not counted twice; any payslip figures you enter; and income sources Nami found in your bank transactions, named after the counterparty on them.
  • Net Wealth: accounts you add yourself, the payments you record against them, any repayment plan you set, and a daily record of your net wealth so the chart can show it moving.
  • Ask Nami and the morning brief: anything you ask Nami to remember, a count of the questions you ask each day, and each morning's brief once it is written. Your chats themselves stay on your phone and are not uploaded, unless you ask Nami to look an earlier one up.
  • Subscription status: if you purchase a subscription, RevenueCat records your purchase and renewal status against the account identifier described above, so we can unlock paid features. Apple processes the payment itself; we never see your card details.
  • Notification tokens: if you allow notifications, Apple issues a token that identifies this installation of Nami on your device, and further tokens for each Lock Screen card. We store them against your account so that notifications can be delivered and the cards kept current. Nami removes them when you sign out, if your phone is online at the time; a token left behind is removed when Apple reports that it no longer works, and every token goes with everything else if you delete your account.
  • Rota feed link, only if you add one: if your rota is a published calendar feed and you paste its link into Nami, we store the link, the words your shift titles start with, and the start and end times of the events in it that match them. Our server fetches the whole feed itself about every five minutes to tell you when your rota changes, so everything in the feed, titles included, passes through our server each time it is read. It matches your shifts by the start of their titles, keeps only their start and end times, and keeps no event titles. You can remove the link at any time.

Nami does not ask for your phone number, location, contacts or photo library, and it never sees a full payment card number.

Your calendar. If you use shift pay, Nami reads the calendar on your phone to work out what your shifts earn. The titles, locations, notes and attendees of the events in the calendar on your phone never leave your device. A rota feed you add is the one exception, because our server reads the published feed itself, as described above. Shift times do leave your phone, in four places, each described in this policy: the worked shifts saved to your account (above); the start and end times of your coming shifts, filed for the week ahead while the Shifts and earnings notifications are on, so the shift card on your Lock Screen can start and keep up while the app is closed, and deleted a day after each shift ends; the shift times sent to Anthropic when you ask Nami about your pay; and, if you add one, your rota feed.

Tap to Log. Tap to Log works only if you build a Shortcuts automation yourself, on the Wallet trigger, which hands Nami the amount and the merchant name each time you pay with a card in Apple Wallet using Apple Pay. Nami does not read your card or Apple Pay, and it holds no Wallet or Apple Pay permission. A purchase logged this way is kept on your phone only, never uploaded, at most twenty at a time. It stays until your bank's own copy of the purchase arrives, or for three days if none does, and then Nami removes it.

The shared merchant book. When a purchase you logged with Tap to Log is paired with your bank's copy of it, your phone sends our server the two merchant names involved (the bank's description, with every word that holds a digit removed, and the shop's name) and nothing else: no account, no amount, no card, no date and no time. We do not record who sent a pair. For each pair our server keeps how many separate days it has been sent on and the first and last of those days. So that one reader counts once a day, the server also keeps, for that day only, a scrambled code made from your account and a random key that exists for that day alone; the codes and the key are deleted once the day is over, when the next day's first pair arrives. A pair is shared with other readers' phones only after it has been sent on at least three separate days, and any pair can be taken down by hand so it is never shared again. This lets the next reader's purchase at the same shop be named straight away. It happens only for purchases logged with Tap to Log, so if you never set up Tap to Log, or turn it off, your phone makes no new pairs to send.

How You Sign In

Nami offers three sign-in methods. You choose which one to use:

  • Email and password: handled by Supabase Auth. Your password is never seen by us in plain text; Supabase stores a salted hash.
  • Sign in with Apple: Apple authenticates you and returns an identity token and your email address, which may be a private relay address you control. Nami exchanges the identity token for a Supabase session, and your account keeps the email address and Apple's identifier for you. Apple may offer Nami your name on your first sign-in; Nami does not keep it.
  • Sign in with Google: Google authenticates you via the official Google Sign-In iOS SDK and returns an identity token carrying your email address, your Google account ID, your name and your profile picture. Nami exchanges the identity token for a Supabase session, and Supabase stores those details with your account. See the Google Privacy Policy for how Google handles your account.

Whichever method you choose, once you have signed in our analytics and our subscription provider know you by the opaque account identifier, never by your email address or your name.

Connecting a Bank (Open Banking)

Connecting a bank is optional, and it is part of Nami Pro. Nami works without it, using statements you upload and purchases you log with Tap to Log. If you do connect one, the connection is made through TrueLayer, a UK open banking provider authorised and regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority. Here is exactly what happens:

  • You authorise it at your own bank. Nami hands you to TrueLayer, which hands you to your bank's own website or app, where you log in and approve the connection. Your banking credentials are entered at your bank and are never seen, handled or stored by Nami.
  • What we receive: the names and types of your accounts, their balances, your cards in masked form (the card's name and its last four digits), your transactions (date, description, merchant name and amount), the name of your bank and the date your consent expires.
  • Read-only, and no ability to move money. The access we ask for is account information only. Nami cannot make a payment, move money between accounts, or change anything at your bank. The connection can only read.
  • We deliberately do not store the account holder name. Your bank can return the name it holds on file for you; Nami stores that field empty, by design, so it never enters our database.
  • When you refresh while you are looking: our server passes your device's IP address to TrueLayer with the request, as open banking rules expect, so your bank can tell that you are present. Scheduled reads made while you are away carry no IP address.
  • How long the connection lasts: your bank sets the expiry date on the consent you give it. Nami records the date your bank returns, and once it passes the connection shows as needing reconnection in the app. We cannot extend or renew it ourselves. Only re-authorising at your bank does that, from Profile, Connected banks, Reconnect.
  • If Pro ends: when a free trial ends without a subscription, or a subscription lapses, your connection is paused. Nami stops reading from your bank, everything it has already brought stays in your account and readable, and the connection's tokens stay on our server so that it can resume. If you subscribe again while your bank's consent stands, the connection resumes without a new consent; if the consent has expired, you are asked to reconnect.
  • Where the keys to the connection live: the access and refresh tokens issued for your connection are held on our server, in a table that no app user and no client can read; only our backend service itself can. They are never sent to your device and never shared with anyone else.
  • How to disconnect: Profile, Connected banks, Disconnect. That removes the whole connection from Nami: its accounts and balances, and the stored access and refresh tokens, so nothing further can be fetched from your bank. Transactions already synced stay in your account, and so does the daily record of your net wealth, which includes that bank's balances up to the day you disconnected; deleting your account removes those along with everything else. Disconnecting in Nami does not withdraw the consent itself: Nami does not ask TrueLayer or your bank to cancel it, so it stands at both until it expires or you withdraw it at your bank.

For how TrueLayer handles the data it passes between your bank and Nami, see the TrueLayer privacy notice.

Third-Party AI Processing

Nami uses a third-party AI service in four places: to categorise transactions from a connected bank, to read statements you upload, to answer the questions you ask Ask Nami, and to write your morning brief. Here is exactly what happens:

  • What is sent from a connected bank: only the transactions our own rules could not categorise on their own, and for each one only five fields: an opaque row reference, the direction ("debit" or "credit"), the amount in pence, the description text, and the merchant name. We add no name, no email address, no user identifier and no account number to it. The description and merchant name, though, go as your bank wrote them, so a transfer's description can carry a person's name, your own name, or a reference that is an account number.
  • What is sent from a statement you upload: the file itself (PDF or CSV), so the transactions can be read out of it. Your account's name and email address are never added to a request. A statement, though, is sent as it is, so anything printed on it, such as your name or an account number, goes with it. A free account can have one statement read a month, and Pro up to fifty.
  • What is sent when you ask Nami a question through the in-app assistant ("Ask Nami"): your question, the last few messages in that chat, and the figures, merchant names and date range of the window you are reading, together with your currency, your monthly budget, your category limits and your category names. With every question, a list of what is connected: the name of each bank and how many accounts it holds, how many accounts you have entered yourself and how many statements you have imported. When you ask about what you own or owe: your net wealth, your account names (for a card, the name your bank gives it and its last four digits) and what each one holds, and any repayment plan on them with the date it clears. When you ask about your pay: your employer's name, your pay dates, what you have earned and what your payslips came to, and the start and end times of the shifts read from your calendar (the titles of your calendar events are never sent), along with the name and kind of each income source you have added, how each one is doing, what it is expected to pay you next and what each has paid you this month. A source Nami found in your bank transactions is named after the counterparty on the transaction, which is usually a business. Also sent: anything you have asked Nami to remember, and the text of an earlier chat only when you ask Nami to look one up. A free account can ask three questions a day, answered by a smaller Claude model; Pro can ask fifty, answered by a larger one.
  • What is sent for your morning brief: the brief is put together on our server each morning, at the time you choose, from the figures already in your account. If you have agreed to AI processing, one request goes to Claude with the figures the brief shows (yesterday's spending, how many purchases it covered and their categories; any pay landing today, with its amount and who it is from; your net wealth today or its forecast for the month's end) and a few short facts Nami may turn into a tip, such as what your regular monthly charges come to, a category over its limit, how your spending is pacing against your budget, or a payslip that came in below what was expected. Claude writes the words around those figures. A free account's brief is written by a smaller Claude model and a Pro account's by a larger one. If you have not agreed, the brief is still written, with the figures and no AI.
  • How it is sent: through our own secure server. Your device never communicates with the AI provider directly, and the API key stays on our server.
  • Purpose: to extract transaction descriptions, amounts, dates and categories, and to write the answers, tips and readings Nami shows you.
  • Your consent: Nami asks for your consent before any of your data is sent for AI processing, and you must agree before anything is sent. The first time you ask Nami a question, a card in the chat names Anthropic and Claude, lists what is and is not sent, and sends nothing until you tap Agree and ask. Your agreement is recorded against your account on our server and re-checked there before every single request, for bank transactions, statements, questions and the morning brief alike, so no processing can happen without it. You can read the full disclosure again, and turn AI processing off or on, at any time from Profile, Privacy & data, AI processing. With it off, statements cannot be read, Ask Nami cannot answer, bank transactions are categorised by Nami's own rules, and your brief is written without AI.
  • Turning it off needs a connection: the switch takes effect on your phone at once, so statements and Ask Nami stop straight away. Bank categorisation and the morning brief run on our server, and they stop only once your choice reaches it. Nami does not yet try again if that fails, so turn AI processing off while you are online. If you turned it off without a connection, or are unsure it went through, write to us and we will turn it off on our side.
  • Your right to delete: you can delete every transaction Nami holds, along with your account, at any time from Profile, Privacy & data, Delete account. Deletion is permanent once it reaches our server, and is described in full under Data Retention & Deletion.

The AI provider Nami currently uses is Anthropic, PBC, via the Claude API. Anthropic does not use your data to train AI models. It is deleted within 30 days in normal use, unless Anthropic has to keep it longer to enforce its usage rules or by law. We keep the resulting transaction records, memories and briefs in your account so they appear when you reopen the app. For more information, see the Anthropic Privacy Policy.

Supabase Privacy Policy

    • The shift card: the start and end times of the shift, the minute the card is counted to, and light or dark. No pay figure, rate or employer name travels for this card; your phone works out the pay from what it already holds.
    • Notifications written on our server carry their own words. For example: the morning brief's one line, which can be your spending so far this month, what you have earned so far this month, your month end forecast or, on a pay day, your pay and who it is from, either landed or expected along with what it depends on; Your pay landed, with the amount and how much more or less it is than expected; and a budget alert, with what you have spent against your budget.

  • Google Sign-In: used only when you choose to Sign in with Google. Google's iOS SDK handles the sign-in and returns an identity token, your email, your Google account ID, your name and your profile picture, which we exchange for a Supabase session. This is a separate, sign-in-only service and is unrelated to the AI processing described above. Google Privacy Policy
  • RevenueCat: used to manage in-app subscription purchases. RevenueCat receives your opaque Supabase account ID, which is pseudonymous rather than anonymous (it carries no name and no email, but it does identify your account), along with your purchase events from Apple's StoreKit. Before you sign in, it uses a random identifier of its own, which is joined to your account identifier when you sign in. Like any web service, it sees the IP address a request comes from, and its software sends your app and iOS versions. It does not see your name, email, or transaction data. RevenueCat Privacy Policy
  • Merchant logos: the small marks beside shop names. Nami ships a built-in list of UK merchants and their websites, and matches your transactions against it on your device. When a shop is on that list but its icon is not yet on your phone, the app asks Nami's own server for the icon by the shop's website address. When a shop is not on that list, the app sends the shop name, and only the shop name, to Nami's own server, which works out the shop's website by trying likely addresses made from the name. Either way our server fetches the public icon from the site itself or from a public icon service (Google's or DuckDuckGo's), then keeps the icon so it is fetched once for everyone. Your device never contacts those services or those websites, and the requests that reach them carry no account, no amount and no user identifier. A shop's website, once found, is stored without any link to you.
  • PostHog: used for product analytics, through PostHog's EU servers. It receives when the app is installed, updated, opened and put away, which screens and features you use, and the sign-in method you chose. Once you have signed in, events are tagged with your opaque Supabase account ID only, which is pseudonymous rather than anonymous: it carries no name and no email, but it does identify your account. Before you sign in, for example while you go through the first screens, PostHog's software tags events with a random identifier of its own, and joins them to your account identifier when you sign in. No event carries an amount, a merchant, an employer, a bank, an account or card name, a note, a search or the text of a question; figures are sent only as broad bands, counts or yes and no. An event about a category limit or a recategorised transaction carries the category's name when it is one of Nami's built-in categories; one you created is sent only as "custom". PostHog's software adds your device model, iOS version, app version, language and region, time zone, screen size and network type, and, like any web service, sees the IP address a request comes from. Nami's PostHog project is set to discard that address, so it is not stored with your events, though PostHog may first use it to estimate a rough location, such as your country and city. Nami does not send crash reports to PostHog, it does not record your screen, and it does not collect your device's advertising identifier. PostHog Privacy Policy

Data Storage & Security

Your data is stored in Supabase (EU region) with Row Level Security enforced, meaning no other user can access your data. All data is transmitted over HTTPS. The AI provider's API key is stored server-side and never included in the app binary. The tokens behind a bank connection are held in a separate table that no client can read at all; only our backend service itself can. Your Ask Nami chats are kept on your phone, protected by iOS data protection and left out of iCloud and computer backups, and purchases logged with Tap to Log are kept on your phone only.

Where your data is processed. Your account and everything stored in it is held in Supabase's EU region, in Stockholm. Our server functions run on Supabase's network, which can handle a request at a location near you on its way to that database. Some of the services named in this policy are based outside the UK and the EU: Anthropic and RevenueCat are in the United States and process what they receive there, and Apple and Google handle sign in and notifications under their own policies. PostHog receives analytics at its EU servers, and TrueLayer is a UK provider. Where your data leaves the UK or the EU, it does so under the data protection terms those providers offer their business customers.

Data Retention & Deletion

Your data is retained for as long as you have an account. You can delete your account at any time from Profile, Privacy & data, Delete account. Deleting your account permanently removes the data Nami holds against it from our database, including your transactions and statements, your budgets and settings, your income, shifts and rota feed, your Net Wealth accounts and history, your memories and briefs, your notification tokens, and any connected bank with the access tokens behind it, and cannot be undone. Our database is backed up daily and each backup is kept for about a week, so a deleted account can remain in those backups until they roll over. If you would rather keep your account, Delete all statements in the same section removes every uploaded statement and its transactions.

Both need a connection. Nami signs you out after Delete account whether or not our server received the request, and clears your statements from the screen before the deletion reaches our server, and at the moment it does not tell you if either failed. So delete while you are online. If you can sign back in with the same details after deleting your account, or a statement comes back the next time you open Nami, the deletion did not go through: do it again, or write to us and we will do it for you.

A few things are not held by us against your account, and deleting it does not reach them. RevenueCat keeps its own record of your purchases under your account identifier, and Apple keeps its record of your subscription; deleting your account does not cancel a subscription, so cancel it first in the Settings app. Analytics events already sent to PostHog stay under the opaque account identifier, which no longer matches any account. Merchant pairs in the shared merchant book were never linked to you and stay. Data sent to Anthropic is kept as described in the AI Processing section. If you signed in with Apple, deleting your account does not remove Nami from the apps using Sign in with Apple in your Apple Account settings; you can stop using it there. Nami does not ask TrueLayer or your bank to cancel a bank consent, so it stands at both until it expires or you withdraw it at your bank, though Nami can no longer use it once the tokens are deleted.

Some things are kept for a set time. The start and end times of your coming shifts are deleted a day after each shift ends. A purchase logged with Tap to Log stays on your phone until your bank's copy of it arrives, or for three days if none does. The scrambled daily codes behind the shared merchant book are deleted once their day is over.
